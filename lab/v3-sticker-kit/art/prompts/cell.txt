A single sticker for a children's illustrated encyclopedia of the 1980s-1990s: a classic textbook illustration of ONE animal cell, extremely detailed fine ink linework with transparent traditional watercolour washes, warm natural colours, scientifically correct and friendly. No 3D render, no cartoon, no photographic look.

SUBJECT: an EMPTY animal cell, seen from slightly above: a rounded, slightly irregular oval shape like a soft flattened dome, about twice as wide as it is tall in the picture. Its cell membrane is a smooth, clearly drawn double-lined skin around the edge, slightly glossy, with a few tiny channel proteins in it. The inside is filled with pale, slightly greenish-cream translucent jelly (cytoplasm), criss-crossed by a fine web of very thin threads (the cytoskeleton) and a scattering of tiny dots (free ribosomes). The inside is otherwise EMPTY: NO nucleus, NO mitochondria, NO organelles, NO bubbles; they will be added later as separate stickers.

BACKGROUND: one perfectly flat, uniform pure magenta (#FF00FF) everywhere around the cell, edge to edge, with no texture, no gradient, no cast shadow. The whole cell fits in the frame with a clear magenta margin; nothing touches the edge.
ABSOLUTELY NO TEXT: no letters, numbers, labels, arrows or annotation lines anywhere.
